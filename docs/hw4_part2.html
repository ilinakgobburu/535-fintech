Motivation

The biggest losses in this project came from one stock falling while the wheel held it. Fiserv's wheel lost −48.7% in 2024-2025. Hence, I ran the same wheel on all five stocks the mean-reversion screen picked, side by side, to see if spreading it across stocks reduces that risk. The setup was committed (a96ec04) before it was run, and nothing in Part I was chosen based on it.

Each of the five wheels starts with $100,000 at its stock's committed setting and runs exactly as in Part I. Their daily account values are added together into one $500,000 portfolio. The benchmarks are the same five stocks bought and held, $100,000 each, and T-bills. The Cboe PutWrite index was added afterwards, as in Part I.

What it shows
  • Less risk for the same return: The portfolio's return is just the average of the five (+28.8% in 2024-2025). However, its volatility was 9.8% against 15.5% for one wheel on average, and its maximum drawdown was −14.5% against −26.3%.
  • Why: The five wheels' monthly results barely moved together, with a median correlation of 0.12 in 2024-2025 and −0.09 in 2026. A wheel loses when its own stock falls through the strike, and that happened at different times for different stocks.
  • One collapse, absorbed: Fiserv's wheel lost −48.7% in 2024-2025, but it only cost the portfolio about a fifth of that.
  • Sharpe ratio: 0.88 in 2024-2025, against 0.55 if the five had moved together (1.55 against 0.99 in 2026). The average of the five single-stock Sharpe ratios (1.43) is higher, but averaging Sharpe ratios gives a calm wheel like Mastercard's the same weight as a volatile one, so it is misleading here.
  • Against holding the stocks: In 2024-2025 the portfolio made +28.8% against +8.4% for holding the five (Sharpe 0.88 against 0.04). In 2026 holding won on return, +28.4% against +14.0%, while the Sharpe ratios were about the same (1.59 against 1.55).
  • Why holding won in 2026: The wheel only owns a stock after a put is assigned, and once it does, the covered call caps the gain. Hence, it misses most of a big rally. The $71,951 gap comes almost entirely from APA and ADI. Held, APA returned +96.8% while its wheel made +33.4%, a $63,391 difference on its own, and ADI returned +45.6% against +15.4%. Fiserv went the other way: held, it returned −27.5%, while its wheel returned −2.8%. This is the tradeoff of the strategy. It gives up big rallies in exchange for smaller losses, which is also why it beat holding in 2024-2025.
