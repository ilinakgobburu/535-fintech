So what would you actually get filled at?

The assignment poses this and answers "you do not know." That is right — but the size of the not-knowing is measurable, because the same pull that gives a mark also gives the BID and ASK it sits between. The mark is a point estimate. The spread is its error bar.

How wrong is the interpolated sheet, really?

Rather than assert that filling holes is dangerous, measure it. Every cell that is observed gets hidden in turn, rebuilt by linear interpolation from its neighbors, and compared against the truth.

These errors are a best case. Each tested cell is an interior point ringed by real observations. The holes you would actually want to fill sit in the wings with far less support around them, so the true error is worse than what this measures.

The underlying, for context

Method & honest caveats

  • Sparsity here is a lower bound. A "listed series" is a (date, RIC) carrying at least one of the two fields. A contract that was listed but had neither a quote nor a print leaves no trace in the pull, so it cannot appear in the denominator. The real holes are at least this bad, probably worse.
  • The sheet stops where the data stops. Interpolation is Delaunay-linear, which returns nothing outside the convex hull of the observations, and grid nodes further than $1.25 in strike-space from any real observation are blanked as well. A wide interior hole stays a hole instead of becoming a smooth ramp.
  • Every view is computed once, in Python. The browser picks a precomputed slice rather than recomputing it, so this page and the Reflex app cannot disagree about a number.
  • Color is never the only encoding. The mark draws as a circle, the trade as a diamond. The palette was checked against the plot surface with a contrast and color-vision validator rather than chosen by eye.
