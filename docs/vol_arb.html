The two-sided variance trade

Options price a stock's downside and its upside separately. This strategy compares each side's price with what the stock actually did on that side, sells the side that is too expensive, buys the cheap side and hedges the direction away, so that its profit comes from implied against realized volatility. The rules were registered before any of this strategy's quantities were computed; the results below are from development, apart from section 7, which runs the same rules on NVDA alone over 2024–26 and reports them without judging them.

1 · The idea

Each side of the smile against its own realized moves

The trade is built so that its profit or loss comes mainly from one question: were each side's options priced above or below the stock's own moves on that side?

A put pays when the stock falls and a call pays when it rises, so the market prices the two directions separately. Specifically, the puts' prices imply how much downside movement the market expects to the expiry, and the calls' prices imply how much upside. The stock's own recent history gives the realized counterpart of each: the variance of its down days and the variance of its up days.

When one side is expensive against its realized moves and the other is cheap, the trade sells the expensive side and buys the cheap one. Both options are sized to about the same vega, so the trade starts with roughly no exposure to a parallel rise or fall in implied volatility, and the stock's direction is hedged away with shares every day. Hence, what is left to earn is mainly the gap between implied and realized volatility on each side, plus any change in the skew itself (the implied-vol part of the split in section 6), not where the stock went.

  1. MeasureEach side's implied variance to the expiry, from that side's options alone (method 2, below).
  2. CompareAgainst the stock's realized down-day and up-day variance over the last 63 returns.
  3. TradeSell the rich side's 0.25-delta option and buy the cheap side's, about $200 of vega each.
  4. HedgeShares offset the options' delta at every open; both legs are held to expiry.
2 · Measuring the premium

Three ways to measure the vol premium, one signal

Method 2 gives each side its own implied variance, so it is the signal; methods 1 and 3 feed the models and check it.

Three methods were built side by side in an earlier pilot so that each could check the others. On SPY from January to April 2020 (four months, one crash), their measures of crash protection moved together (r = 0.97). Hence, here they are not three separate signals: method 2 is the signal and methods 1 and 3 are inputs and cross-checks. Method 2 is the signal because it is the only one that gives each side its own implied variance, in the same units as the realized down-day and up-day variance it is compared with.

The three methods

The strike corridor. Each side's variance uses only the quoted strikes within 1.5 standard deviations of the forward, |ln(K/F)| ≤ 1.5 × σATM × √T, at the stock's own at-the-money implied vol on the entry day. Because the corridor scales with each stock's vol and each cycle's time to expiry, a calm stock and a wild one are measured over comparable parts of their smiles. A stock-cycle with fewer than 3 quoted strikes on either side inside the corridor, or whose quotes stop short of the corridor's end by more than one strike step, is skipped, counted and not traded.

The flat-smile correction. Even a perfectly flat smile does not split evenly between the halves: where the forward falls between two listed strikes moves part of the strip from one half to the other. Hence, f1 and f2 are each netted by the same split computed on a flat smile at σATM, so a flat smile leans neither way. Similarly, the corridor holds about 95% of a flat smile's variance, so f3 reads about −0.05 on a flat smile rather than 0; the offset is the same for every stock and vol level, so the models are unaffected.

3 · The trade

A hedged 0.25-delta put and call, held to expiry

Each cycle sells one 0.25-delta option and buys the other, about $200 of vega each, hedges the stock's direction at every open and holds both legs to expiry.

The rules, as registered

Legs
The put and the call closest to 0.25 |delta| on the cycle's monthly expiry, chosen from every listed strike with a two-sided quote at the entry close.
Direction
Put side rich: sell the put, buy the call. Call side rich: buy the put, sell the call.
Size
Each leg's contracts = round($200 ÷ its vega per vol point per contract), at least 1: about $200 per vol point per leg on a $100,000 book per stock.
Timing
Entered at the close of the first session after each monthly expiry and held to the next monthly expiry, where both legs settle at intrinsic value and the shares are closed.
Hedge
Shares offset the two legs' delta at the entry close, then are rebalanced at each later open, up to and including the expiry's, from the previous close's deltas. Dividends are paid or received on shares held over an ex-date.
Fills
Mid: both legs at the entry close's mid. Stress: sell at the bid, buy at the ask, and 5 bp of every hedge trade's value.
Book
The P&L is an overlay: a month's return is its P&L ÷ $100,000, with no T-bill subtracted. The pooled book adds the stocks' P&L over the number of stocks × $100,000. A month with no trade counts as 0.

Sanji, the course server, fills at the next open. The backtest fills the options at the close instead, because the cache holds closing quotes only; the hedges trade at each open.

4 · How the model decides

A ladder from the simplest rule to a shallow forest

Four rungs, from always selling the put side to a shallow random forest, each decide which side is rich; a more complex candidate reaches the final test only by beating the simpler ones in development.

Each rung decides, at every measured stock-cycle, whether the put side or the call side is rich. The rungs go from no model at all to a random forest, and a more complex candidate is sent to the test only if it beats the simpler candidates (the forest must beat rule 1 and the logistic regression; the logistic regression must beat rule 1). Rule 0 is not a candidate: it is the bar the test must clear. The models predict the label: 1 if the put-rich trade made money over the cycle at mid, else 0.

The band. A model trades the put side when p > 0.55, the call side when p < 0.45, and nothing in between, so a model that is unsure stays out.

The walk-forward. At each entry date, in order, each model is fit on every stock-cycle that had already finished (its expiry before that date) and predicts the five stocks' cycles entered that day. A cycle enters the training set only after it has finished, so no row's own cycle, or any later one, is ever in its training set. Predictions start once the finished rows span at least 24 entry months; every rung, the two rules included, is scored over the same months from then. Specifically, the first prediction was made on January 19, 2021 from 77 finished rows, and the last on November 20, 2023 from 200.

The choice for the test. On development's out-of-sample months, the pooled Sharpe ratio at mid of rule 1, the logistic regression and the forest is compared. The forest is sent to the final test only if its Sharpe ratio is above both others'; otherwise the higher of rule 1 and the logistic regression is sent, a tie going to rule 1. Rule 0 is never sent: it is the bar.

5 · How it is tested honestly

Rules first, one development run, one final test

Every rule was committed before the data it governs was computed; development and the final test use different years and, apart from NVDA, different stocks; and the final test may run only once (it has not been run: decision of November 16, 2026).

Specifically, the question and the data were fixed before the pull; the inputs, trade, models and pass bar before any of this strategy's implied variances, features, labels or P&L were computed on a real chain; and every later change is a dated amendment or clarification, each made before any run on real data.

The pass bar. The rung sent to the test passes only if its pooled test Sharpe ratio at the stress fill is above 0, its Sharpe ratio at mid is above rule 0's at mid, and its Sharpe ratio at the stress fill is above rule 0's at the stress fill (like with like). If any of these four Sharpe ratios cannot be computed, it is not a pass. Reported but not judged: whether its test Sharpe ratio at mid is at least 1.2 (about t = 2 over 33 months), NVDA alone, every rung's figures, per stock and per year.

How much a pass would mean. The six test stocks share the market's months, so the test has about 33 separate months, not about 198 independent stock-months. Hence, a pass would mean the idea is worth pursuing, not that it is proven.

Four thin stocks dropped. On NVDA alone, January 2024 to October 2026, the logistic regression, refit at each entry as the final test would refit it, had a Sharpe ratio of +0.18 at mid and −0.18 at the stress fill, against rule 0's −0.52 and −0.86; fit once on the development rows instead, as the vol-arb nightly script (lib/vol_arb_live.py) fits it, the same model did better: +0.50 and +0.13.

Reported, not judged. NVDA alone, run on request after the development run. It is not a pass or fail, and it is not the final test: the registered six-stock final test stays not run, and no other test stock's 2024–26 data was read.

What was run. The registered rules, unchanged (the same measurement, nine features, band, legs, size, daily hedge and fills), on NVDA alone and one $100,000 book: its 33 monthly cycles from January 22, 2024 to the October 16, 2026 expiry (29 measured; 4 skipped because the quotes stopped short of the corridor's end), then the cycle entered October 19, 2026, marked at the November 16 close. Why now. NVDA's recent swings: since October 19 it has moved with a realized volatility of about 59%, against the 39% its at-the-money options implied that day, and by November 16 it had fallen 28%.

9 · Caveats

What the backtest cannot see

The backtest fills options at closing quotes, sees only a corridor of strikes, knows earnings dates after the fact and tests stocks whose options stayed quoted.

Limits of the data and the design

Closing quotes, not open fills
Every option fill here is at the entry close's quotes, because the cache has closing quotes only; the hedges trade at each open. Sanji fills options at the next open, and the stress fill (the spread plus 5 bp on hedges) cannot show how far prices move overnight.
The strike corridor
Each side's variance counts only the strikes inside 1.5 standard deviations that were quoted on both bid and ask, and only those inside the pull's bands (puts 0.70–1.02, calls 0.97–1.40 times the stock's price around the previous expiry). Hence, it is a corridor version that leaves out the far tails, the same way for every stock and both periods.
Earnings dates as actual dates
Input f7 uses the earnings dates in the cache, which are the dates the companies actually reported. Live, only the expected date is known in advance, so the nightly script must use that instead.
Survivorship of the chosen stocks
The stocks come from sets cached for earlier work, and four were dropped for thin quotes found in the test window itself (strike coverage only, no result). Hence, the test covers only stocks whose options stayed quoted widely enough through 2024–26. It does include FISV's 2025 fall, but it says nothing about stocks whose options thinned out, and the results may flatter the strategy for that reason.
No early exercise
Both legs are held to expiry and settled at intrinsic value; early exercise and assignment are not modelled in the backtest, though a live short put can be assigned early.
Few separate months
Development scores 36 calendar months and the test about 33. The stocks share those months, so a single bad month for the market moves every book at once.
