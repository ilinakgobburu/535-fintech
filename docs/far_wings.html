Selling the far wings, delta-hedged

A nightly options algorithm for the course server. It is set up to sell, once a month, one far out-of-the-money put and one far call on NVDA and on each of 30 large stocks, and to hedge each stock with its shares only when its exposure leaves a band. Its first entry is a one-off on Wednesday, November 18, 2026, on all 31 stocks, and from December 21 it enters monthly. The course server does not fill option orders yet. Its one final test, on 2024–26 data, did not pass the bar set before it ran (section 3); the script stays live as an exploration, not a tested edge.

What it trades
NVDA and a fixed basket of 30: the 30 largest S&P 500 companies by market value on December 31, 2018, one share class each (MSFT, AAPL, AMZN, GOOG, BRK B, META, JNJ, JPM, V, XOM, WMT, PFE, UNH, BAC, VZ, PG, WFC, INTC, CVX, T, KO, MRK, MA, CSCO, HD, BA, DIS, ORCL, PEP, CMCSA). It used ten stocks (five in development, six in the final test, NVDA in both); DIS in development and MA and V in the final test are the only basket names among them.
Entry nights
The first session after each monthly expiry. For each stock it sells the put nearest 0.05 |delta| (among puts under 0.10) and the call nearest 0.03 (among calls under 0.05), each with a limit order at the closing mid, round($200 ÷ the option's vega per vol point) contracts: about $200 of vega per wing.
Other nights
While a stock's options are held, it checks that stock's net delta × its close. Above $5,000 (5% of its book), a market order for the next open (two when the shares cross zero) hedges it back to zero; after an expiry the leftover shares are closed at the next open. Otherwise it writes [].

1 · Why this strategy

Why the far wings Exploratory · data already seen

Options are usually priced above the moves that follow, and on single stocks most of the put side's extra implied variance sits far out of the money. Sold delta-hedged, the far wings earned the most before costs; after costs no part of the smile clearly paid.

On the monthly option chains of NVDA, AMD, DIS, COST and DAL in 2019–23, a median 63% of the put side's implied variance above a flat smile at the at-the-money vol sat in puts under 0.10 delta (58 to 67% by stock). Each delta zone was then sold at $100 of vega per vol point on each of the 295 stock-cycles where it had options, and delta-hedged daily. Before costs the far wings earned the most: calls under 0.05 delta +⁠$378 a cycle, puts under 0.05 delta +⁠$260 and puts at 0.05–0.10 delta +⁠$182, against +⁠$37 for a single 0.25-delta put (calls under 0.05 delta led on only two of the five stocks, COST and DAL). After costs no zone clearly paid; the bid-ask spread was the main cost. These looks used the development data, so they shaped the choice of strategy and are not evidence that it works.

2 · How it works

How it works

Two wings
On the first session after each monthly expiry, for each stock, it sells two options of the next monthly expiry: the put whose |delta| is closest to 0.05 among puts under 0.10 (a tie to the lower strike) and the call closest to 0.03 among calls under 0.05 (a tie to the higher strike). A wing with no eligible strike sits the cycle out.
Size
round($200 ÷ the option's Black-76 vega per vol point per contract), at least one contract: about $200 per vol point per wing, on each stock's $100,000 book.
Limit at the mid
Each option is sold with a limit at the entry close's mid, for the next session. The backtest's headline limit fill fills it at that price only if the next session's closing mid is at least the limit, and otherwise leaves the wing out for the cycle; its hedge trades at no cost. The stress fill sells at the bid, always filled, and pays 5 bp on every hedge trade.
Delta band
Each night, the stock's net delta in shares (each option's delta from its closing mid, plus the shares held). Only when |net delta × close| is above $5,000 (5% of the book) are shares traded at the next open, back to zero delta.
Hold to expiry
The options settle at intrinsic value at the expiry close, and the hedge shares are closed then. There is no crash cap: the hedge is the only protection.

The probabilistic Sharpe ratio is the probability that the true Sharpe ratio is above zero, given the number of months and the returns' skewness and kurtosis. The deflated one raises that bar to the best Sharpe ratio N trials would show by luck, from the development trials' variance. N = 7 counts the trials registered when each stage ran: four rules and models of an earlier design, the 30-stock book's two versions and this study's one position; 18 adds two drafts' 11 variants, which never ran. The registered bar uses the plain Sharpe ratios above; these are reported.

5 · Caveats

What these numbers cannot show

The development data chose the strategy, so only the final test judges, and it did not pass; 27 of the 30 basket names were never in the backtest, nothing caps a gap between two nights, and the backtest's limit fill is stricter than the course server's.

Eight limits of the data and the design

Development is data already seen
The far wings were picked from looking at the same 2019–23 chains development uses, so development is a gate, not evidence. Only the 2024–26 test judges. That window is fresh for this position, but earlier unhedged put studies used five of its six stocks, NVDA's options there had been computed on before the test, and its crash days were known in advance.
The basket is untested
27 of the 30 basket names were never in the backtest (DIS, MA and V were). What they do live is out of sample in names as well as in time, and the backtest that included them did not pass.
No crash cap
The hedge is the only protection, and a gap between two nights is not hedged at all, whether a fall (the short puts) or a rally (the short calls). In the final test FISV's cycle entered October 20, 2025, lost 117% of its $100,000 book at the stress fill. The registration's synthetic probes put one instantaneous 30% fall at a loss of about $36,000 to $160,000 on one stock's book.
The limit fill
The backtest fills a limit at the mid only when the next session's closing mid is at least the limit, which is when the price has not moved the seller's way (section 3). The live rules expect the course server to fill a limit sell when the session's high reaches it, a looser rule, so live fills would come more often than backtest fills; and it does not fill option orders yet.
The band saves little
In development it cut hedge trades from 5,919 (hedging daily) to 3,951, but saved only 3.2% of daily hedging's cost.
Few months
The final test has 34 calendar months, so a Sharpe ratio there has a standard error of about 0.59, and more for skewed returns like a short option's.
The strike band
The option data stop at a strike band: puts from 0.70 and calls up to 1.40 times the stock's closes around the previous expiry. In high-vol months a wing then takes the nearest strike inside the band, nearer the money than its target, or the call wing finds none and sits the cycle out; in the backtest and live alike.
Early assignment
These are American options: the course server assigns a short option on any close through its strike, and the backtest has no early exercise.
