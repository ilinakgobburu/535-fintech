Selling the far wings, delta-hedged

A nightly options algorithm for the course server. Once a month it sells one far out-of-the-money put and one far call on NVDA and on each of 30 large stocks, and it hedges each stock with shares only when its exposure leaves a band.

Before any order, the run checks its settings, the order and fill files and the clock, and writes nothing if anything is off, so a late start never sends a stale order.

What it trades
NVDA and the 30 largest S&P 500 companies by market value on December 31, 2018 (MSFT, AAPL, AMZN, GOOG, BRK B, META, JNJ, JPM, V, XOM, WMT, PFE, UNH, BAC, VZ, PG, WFC, INTC, CVX, T, KO, MRK, MA, CSCO, HD, BA, DIS, ORCL, PEP, CMCSA), each its own $100,000 book.
Every night
Entry nights too: while options are held, if |net delta × close| tops $5,000 (5% of the book), a market order for the next open hedges back to zero; with no options left (after an expiry or an assignment), any leftover shares are closed. With no order for any stock, it writes [].
First entry
A one-off for Wednesday, November 18, 2026, chosen from the November 17 close into the December 18, 2026 expiry (31 days instead of the tested 25), in place of that cycle's November 23 entry. Then monthly: the next entry is chosen from the December 21, 2026 close (orders for December 22).

1 · Why this strategy

Why the far wings

Options are usually priced above the moves that follow, and on single stocks most of the put side's extra implied variance sits far out of the money.

How the far wings were found. Three ways of splitting an option's price, first tried on SPY's 2020 option chains:

  1. Protection: VIX minus at-the-money implied vol, the part of the price that pays for out-of-the-money options.
  2. Puts against calls: the variance VIX prices, split into its puts' and its calls' share, each set against the down days and up days that followed.
  3. The smile's shape: a parabola fitted to implied vol across strikes, split into its tilt (puts dearer than calls) and its bend (both wings raised).

Applied zone by zone to the monthly chains of NVDA, AMD, DIS, COST and DAL in 2019–23, they put a median 63% of the put side's extra implied variance in puts under 0.10 delta, and showed that on single stocks the bend, not the tilt, carries most of it, so the far calls are priced up too. Hence two far wings: a put near 0.05 delta and a call near 0.03. Sold delta-hedged, zone by zone, the far wings earned the most before costs (calls under 0.05 delta +⁠$378 a cycle per $100 of vega, against +⁠$37 for a 0.25-delta put); after costs, mostly the bid-ask spread, no zone clearly paid. These looks used the development data, so they chose the strategy but are not evidence that it works.

2 · How it was backtested

How the backtest trades it

Rules
The nightly script's, above: the put nearest 0.05 and the call nearest 0.03 |delta| (ties to the lower put strike and the higher call strike), about $200 of Black-76 vega each, on a $100,000 book per stock, hedged at the next open only outside the $5,000 band.
Two fills
The headline limit fill sells at the entry close's mid only if the next session's closing mid is at least that limit, otherwise the wing sits out, and hedges cost nothing. The stress fill sells at the bid, always filled, and pays 5 bp a hedge trade.
Hold to expiry
Options settle at intrinsic value at the expiry close and the hedge shares close then. No crash cap: the hedge is the only protection.

The probabilistic Sharpe ratio is the probability that the true Sharpe ratio is above zero, given the number of months and the returns' skewness and kurtosis. The deflated one raises that bar to the best Sharpe ratio N trials would show by luck, from the development trials' variance. N = 7 counts the trials registered when each stage ran: four rules and models of an earlier design, the 30-stock book's two versions and this study's one position; 18 adds two drafts' 11 variants, which never ran. The registered bar uses the plain Sharpe ratios above; these are reported.

4 · Why it failed

Why it failed

Most cycles earned their premium. About one in eight held a gap the hedge could not follow, and those cycles lost more than all the others made.

Gaps
25 of the 198 stock-cycles (13%) had a day that moved at least 3 times what the options implied. At the stress fill they lost $453,818; the other 173 made $84,863 (limit fill: −⁠$153,908 against +⁠$108,920).
The hedge, not expiry
Only 7 of the 359 wings sold finished in the money. The losses came from the share hedge, which trades at the next open and so follows a gap a day late.
Costs
Selling at the bid gave up 40% of the wings' mid premium in the test, against 12% in development.
The puts
At the stress fill the put wings lost $357,173 and the calls $19,171.

What it changed. These findings became rules of a follow-up study: a cap on what a 3-times gap could cost each stock, a filter that skips options whose bid-ask spread is more than half their mid, and earnings inside the cycle as a known risk. Its test has not run yet.

A regrouping of the final test's own per-cycle records after the verdict: it changes nothing about the verdict and is not a test. A gap: a close-to-close move of at least 3 times the entry's implied daily move.
