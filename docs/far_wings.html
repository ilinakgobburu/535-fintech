Selling the far wings, held to expiry

A nightly options algorithm for the course server. Once a month it sells one far out-of-the-money put and one far call on each stock and holds them to expiry, with no hedge. The backtest ran on five stocks in development (2019–23) and six in the final test (2024–26); the live run, which tests it on the course server, trades the same rules on NVDA and 30 large stocks, 27 of them never backtested.

1 · Why this strategy

Why the far wings

Options are usually priced above the moves that follow, and on single stocks most of the put side's extra implied variance sits far out of the money.

How the far wings were found. Three ways of splitting an option's price, first tried on SPY's 2020 option chains:

  1. Protection: VIX minus at-the-money implied vol, the part of the price that pays for out-of-the-money options.
  2. Puts against calls: the variance VIX prices, split into its puts' and its calls' share, each set against the down days and up days that followed.
  3. The smile's shape: a parabola fitted to implied vol across strikes, split into its tilt (puts dearer than calls) and its bend (both wings raised).

Applied zone by zone to the monthly chains of NVDA, AMD, DIS, COST and DAL in 2019–23, they put a median 63% of the put side's extra implied variance in puts under 0.10 delta, and showed that on single stocks the bend, not the tilt, carries most of it, so the far calls are priced up too. Hence two far wings: a put near 0.05 delta and a call near 0.03. Sold delta-hedged, zone by zone, the far wings earned the most before costs (calls under 0.05 delta +⁠$378 a cycle per $100 of vega, against +⁠$37 for a 0.25-delta put); after costs, mostly the bid-ask spread, no zone clearly paid. These looks used the development data and a daily hedge, so they chose the strategy but are not evidence that it works, hedged or not.

2 · How it was backtested

How the backtest trades it

Rules
The nightly script's, above: the put nearest 0.05 and the call nearest 0.03 |delta| (ties to the lower put strike and the higher call strike), about $200 of Black-76 vega each, on a $100,000 book per stock, with no hedge.
Two fills
The headline limit fill sells at the entry close's mid only if the next session's closing mid is at least that limit, otherwise the wing sits out. The stress fill sells at the bid, always filled. The limit fill is the headline because the course server fills nightly orders only in the next session; with a real broker the script could decide and trade just before the close, around 3:45 pm.
Hold to expiry
Options settle at intrinsic value at the expiry close. No hedge and no crash cap: the registered version hedged with shares outside a $5,000 band, and the hedge was removed after its final test (section 4).

4 · Why no hedge

Why no hedge

In the final test the hedge's shares lost more than the options made. In 2020 they saved the book.

The final test
The options alone made +⁠$67,611 at the stress fill (+⁠$190,096 at the limit fill); the hedge's shares lost $436,566 ($235,083). The hedge sells after drops and buys after rises, so it loses when moves reverse, as after the rally of April 9, 2025, and after an overnight gap it trades a day late.
2020
In development the hedge's shares made +⁠$366,200 at the stress fill. Without them Mar 2020 lost 96% of the pooled book, and DAL's cycle entered February 24, 2020 lost 413% of its own; with them the worst month was −21.3% (Feb 2020).

Future research. The hedged version's losses became rules of a follow-up that chose a strangle, condor or other structure each month; the monthly choice failed in 2026, but a fixed iron condor held up. A hedged, put-tilted version of it passed its test on NVDA's weekly options (stress Sharpe 0.99 against a bar of 0.5), and a year-long forward walk is next.
