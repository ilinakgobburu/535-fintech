Part I

The assignment: the wheel on Mastercard

Everything the assignment asks for: the thesis and whether it held, the blotter, the ledger, the portfolio on any date, and the evidence for the fill prices.
Thesis

The wheel sells a cash-secured put each month and, once assigned, a covered call against the shares. It was expected to improve the Sharpe ratio relative to holding Mastercard, for two reasons. First, the strategy is short options, and options are typically priced above the moves that follow, so the premium collected should exceed what the options cost on average. Second, the wheel buys after a fall (assignment) and sells after a rise (called away); hence, it is a long-only mean-reversion position and should do best on a stock whose monthly moves tend to reverse. Though the short call caps the upside, the return was expected to be close to the stock's with considerably less risk.

The improvement in Sharpe ratio held, though not for the reason Mastercard was chosen. Specifically, implied volatility was above the volatility that followed in 9 of 9 test cycles, but Mastercard's monthly moves no longer reversed in 2024-2026: its variance ratio rose from 0.57 to 0.91, close to the 0.93 ± 0.25 of a random walk over the same number of months. Hence, the lower risk came from the structure of the wheel, cash earning T-bill interest while each put is open plus the premium collected, rather than from mean reversion.

Portfolio on any date
Pick any session to see the account as it stood at that day's close. The open option is marked at that day's mid, or at its last mid when no two-sided quote exists.

Every option is held to expiry by rule, so the blotter contains no rolls and no early buy-backs: each position closes by expiring, by assignment, or by being called away.

One row per session, at the close. Click a row to see that day in the portfolio panel.
Setting
Puts at 0.15 delta, calls at 0.35 delta, cost-basis floor on; chosen on 2024–25 out of 18 settings
Cycle
Every monthly expiry opens a cycle on the next session, expiring on the next monthly expiry (24 to 35 days out); held to expiry, no rolls, no early close
Cash phase
With no shares, sell puts at the strike whose |delta| is closest to the put delta, as many contracts as the cash already in the account secures at the strike (contracts x 100 x strike); a delta tie goes to the lower strike
Share phase
With shares, sell one call per 100 shares at the strike whose delta is closest to the call delta; with the floor on, only strikes at or above the cost basis; a tie goes to the higher strike; if no quoted strike qualifies, hold the shares with no call that cycle
Cost basis
The assigned put's strike, less its premium, less every call premium collected on those shares since
Expiry
Settles on the expiry day's close. A put with the close below its strike is assigned (the shares are bought at the strike); a call with the close above its strike is called away (the shares are sold at the strike); otherwise it expires. Early assignment is not modelled
Quotes
A strike counts only if it has a two-sided quote on the decision date
Volatility
Implied vol inverted from the mid with Black-Scholes; delta from that vol
Rate
3-month T-bill yield, on the decision date
Cash
Earns the 3-month T-bill over the calendar days between closes, collateral included
Dividends
Credited on the ex-date to shares held at the prior close
Earnings
Sold through, for every setting; cycles with an earnings date are flagged in the output
Contracts
Whole real contracts of 100 shares
Fills
Mid of the closing bid and ask; stress test at the bid (the book never buys an option back)
Commissions
None charged
Fill pricesi

The location of a print is (trade − bid) / (ask − bid): 0 is the bid, 0.5 the mid, 1 the ask.

Each option is sold at the mid of the closing bid and ask, and the same book is also run selling at the bid as a stress test. The mid is supported by LSEG's daily data: on the 2,601 days on which a contract like those the wheel sells traded (a monthly, out of the money, |delta| 0.10-0.40, 20-36 days to expiry), the last trade printed at a median of 0.51 of the way from bid to ask, where 0.5 is the mid. A regression of the print on the mid has a slope of 1.004 and an R² of 0.974. Though the median is at the mid, individual prints vary: 44% fell within a quarter-spread of the mid, and 15% printed at or below the bid. Hence, the result at the bid (+9.4%) is shown beside the result at the mid (+10.1%) as a lower bound.

Notes

Timing. The last trade of the day can occur hours before the closing quote it is compared with, so part of the 29% of prints outside the quote reflects the stock moving in between rather than trades outside the market. Size. The wheel sells one or two contracts at a time, against a median daily volume of 24 contracts and open interest of 494 in the band, so it is unlikely to move the price. Assignment. Shares are bought or delivered at the strike, by the terms of the contract, so assignment needs no fill assumption.

Stock choice396 of 469 eligible stocks ranked

Mastercard was chosen by a rule rather than by hand. The S&P 500 as it stood on 29 December 2023 (503 members, including those since removed, so the list is not today's survivors) was ranked by how strongly each stock's monthly moves reversed over 2014-2023, using the variance ratio over three months and the monthly autocorrelation, and a stock was ranked only if both indicated reversal. Mastercard ranked first, with a variance ratio of 0.57 against a median of 0.84. The backtest uses only the years after that window, because testing a stock on the years that selected it would grade it on its own selection.

Setting choicehighlighted: the pick

The put delta, call delta and cost-basis floor were chosen on 2024-2025 by training Sharpe ratio, out of 18 settings, and committed (b310900) before 2026 was run; 2026 was then run once. The pick ranked 9 of 18 in 2026, and its Sharpe ratio (2.55) was close to that of the default setting fixed before any data was seen (2.59). Hence, the choice of setting added little, and the result depends far more on the stock and the period.

Accuracy

The code behind this page is checked by 90 tests. The engine's tests run on a market small enough to verify by hand, including that no decision reads a later row than the one it trades on. Before this page was built, the committed setting was re-run and reproduced all 27 recorded figures exactly.

Data: 1,573 contracts requested from LSEG, 1,361 returned, 212 never listed; 48,277 daily quotes, 31,336 with both a bid and an ask.
Reporting

Each decision uses only that day's closing quotes: the strike is chosen from contracts quoted that day, and expiry settles on that day's close. The stock (29a831b), the setting (b310900) and the test window were each committed before the 2026 test was run.

Limitations

Some limits to these findings include: one stock over a short test of 9 monthly cycles with a single assignment; daily closing quotes rather than executable prices; no commissions; early assignment is not modelled in the main run, though a re-run that models it (a put with no time value left, or a call before an ex-dividend date) gave exactly the same trades; and a final month (1-16 October 2026) that is partial but is charged a full month of T-bill yield in the Sharpe ratio, which lowers every book's Sharpe slightly.
